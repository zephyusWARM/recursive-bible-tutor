# Mathematical Mode

## Purpose before algebra

Before manipulating symbols, establish what problem the equation solves and what each object represents.

## Formula protocol

For any unfamiliar formula:

1. Read it aloud in words.
2. Define each symbol and its type/domain.
3. Identify what is fixed versus variable.
4. State the relationship qualitatively.
5. Inspect the shape: monotone? symmetric? bounded? exponential? linear?
6. Explain why that structure is plausible for the problem.
7. Derive it from previously established facts.
8. Substitute a minimal numerical example.
9. Check simple/extreme values.
10. Contrast with a nearby wrong formula.

## Derivation discipline

For every nontrivial line change, answer:

- what rule was used;
- why its assumptions hold;
- what changed algebraically;
- what the step means conceptually.

If a derivation relies on a theorem the learner has not mastered, recurse to that prerequisite instead of hiding it.

## Probability-specific warning

Keep these objects distinct:

- event;
- probability of an event;
- conditional probability;
- random variable;
- realized value;
- PMF/PDF/CDF;
- joint versus marginal distributions.

Many apparent algebra problems are actually object-confusion problems.

## Sanity checks

Use checks appropriate to the object:

- probabilities stay in [0,1];
- distributions normalize;
- dimensions/units agree;
- limits behave plausibly;
- symmetry appears where expected;
- zero/noise/large-sample special cases reduce correctly.
