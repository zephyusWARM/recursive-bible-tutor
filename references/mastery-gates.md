# Mastery Gates

## Purpose

Prevent “I followed the explanation” from being mislabeled as durable knowledge.

## Evidence hierarchy

Use this rough hierarchy from weakest to strongest:

1. **Recognition** — learner says the explanation looks familiar.
2. **Guided completion** — learner finishes a heavily scaffolded example.
3. **Recall** — learner produces the idea from memory.
4. **Application** — learner uses it on a fresh example.
5. **Discrimination** — learner distinguishes it from a plausible near-neighbor.
6. **Transfer** — learner solves a structurally related problem with changed surface features.
7. **Generation** — learner creates an example, counterexample, explanation, or derivation.

Do not use levels 1–2 as sufficient mastery evidence.

## Four default gates

### Gate A — Reconstruction

Ask for a self-generated explanation, derivation skeleton, or definition. Do not accept copied wording as strong evidence.

### Gate B — Application

Change the numbers, objects, or setting while preserving the structure.

### Gate C — Discrimination

Offer a plausible wrong statement or a neighboring concept. Ask which applies and why.

### Gate D — Transfer

Change the surface representation, combine with a prior concept, or remove a familiar cue.

A concept can become MASTERED when the learner passes most gates with low scaffolding. Hard topics may require all four.

## Scaffolding ladder

When the learner succeeds, remove support:

`worked example → partial hints → cue only → no hint → changed context → open generation`

When the learner fails, add the minimum support needed. Avoid instantly returning to a full lecture.

## Delayed retrieval

A concept that passes immediately may still be FRAGILE. Re-test later. Retrieval after delay is stronger evidence than immediate repetition.

## Error interpretation

A wrong answer may mean:

- missing prerequisite;
- notation failure;
- procedural slip;
- overgeneralization;
- wrong causal/mechanistic model;
- retrieval failure despite prior understanding.

Diagnose which one before reteaching.
